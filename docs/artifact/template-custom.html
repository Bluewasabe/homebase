Homebase Custom Three

Three front doors written from a blank file, each borrowing its whole visual language from something in your world instead of from a dashboard product. All three are live on hub right now, reading the same feed, Uptime Kuma, Proxmox and Plex as the stock variants, plus a new sidecar that does a real Minecraft and Bedrock handshake for player counts. Every screenshot is the real page.

What they share under the hood

One nginx serves all three and proxies every data source on the same origin, so the pages hold no secrets and need no CORS. A Python sidecar answers /mc/java and /mc/bedrock with a genuine status handshake, which is the check that would have caught the All the Mons zombie in September. The service list is generated from the same inventory file as every other variant, and the checker passes for all nine.

The three

Flight Deck

:3301annunciator panel · engine gauges · nav scope

An aircraft caution panel is the best "is anything wrong" display ever designed: a grid of lettered tiles that stay dark until they matter. Here MASTER CAUTION and MASTER WARNING sit at the top, with DISK LOW, CTR UNHEALTHY, PORT CLOSED, GAME OFFLINE, MONITOR DOWN, FEED STALE, and two green advisories for PLEX ACTIVE and PLAYERS ON. Under it, every host is an engine cluster with three round gauges drawn on canvas, red arcs where the limits are. The nav scope puts each service as a blip: sector is the machine, ring is the function, and you click a blip to open it.

Type · palette
  • B612 and B612 Mono, the typeface Airbus commissioned for cockpit displays. It is on Google Fonts and it reads at 11 px.
  • Phosphor green, caution amber, warning red, cyan for data, on near-black panels with bezel greys
Signature

  • Gauges have red arcs, so a needle in the red is visible from across a room
Where it wins
  • Fastest fault read of the nine, stock ones included
  • Everything on one screen at 1440, and it stacks cleanly on a phone
Where it loses
  • The scope crowds when one machine has twenty services; it is a picture more than a menu
  • Links live in the checklist column, so it is a two-glance page for "open X"

Rack Elevation

:3302drive bays · LED per container · patch panel

The lab as a literal rack. Each machine is a unit face with a Dymo label, a bank of drive bays that fill as the disk fills and go amber under 20% and red under 10%, one LED per container with players shown in blue where anyone is on, and seven-segment readouts for CPU, memory, worst disk and container count. pve shows its VMs and LXCs as LEDs from the Proxmox API; the UDM unit at the top lights one link per host the feed can reach. The right side is a patch panel: every tool is an RJ45 port, lit when up, grouped by what it is for. The fault log at the top is the only text that is not a label.

Transit Map

:3303one line per machine · stations are tools · departure board

A transit map is a link wall that also shows topology, and everybody already knows how to read one. Each machine is a colored line; each service is a station you click; a service on two machines is an interchange (Ollama, dashed between the Server-Rig and PlexBoy lines). Vertical zones group stations by what they are for, so Games is one stretch of the red line and Infra is where the green pve line gets busy. A station that is down is a red disc with a cross, the way a closed station is marked. The departure board on top lists delays instead of alerts and shows good service when there are none. Light and dark themes both.

Type · palette

  • Line colors follow real transit conventions: red, blue, green, purple, and a dashed grey line for the cloud
Signature

  • The departure board: DELAY in red, GOOD SERVICE in green, in the font of an airport flap board
Where it wins
  • Most legible link wall of all nine; you can hand it to the family
  • Topology and status in one picture; the only one that shows "this runs in two places"
Where it loses
  • Least numeric: no gauges, host stats are in cards below the fold
  • Station labels get tight in the Games zone; at 900 px wide the map scrolls sideways

Honest trade. The customs cost nothing to add a service to (edit the inventory, done) and everything to add a new kind of widget to (you write it). Homepage is the reverse. The customs also have zero upstream: no updates, no CVEs, no breaking config changes, and no one else's roadmap. If one of these is the answer, the practical shape is Flight Deck as the phone and wall page, Rack as the desk page, sharing the same data layer, with Transit kept for the family. If you'd rather not own frontend code, H2 plus H3 remains the right stock answer.
